# Appendix B: Qualitative Protocols and Question Banks

## B.1 Participant Onboarding

A primer was distributed 48 hours before each session, defining the core
constructs participants would be asked about during the think-aloud and
interview: Socratic facilitation (scaffolded rather than direct answers),
document grounding (retrieval-augmented generation over the participant's
own uploaded materials), and local/on-device inference (no cloud
transmission of prompts or documents).

## B.2 Session Structure & Timing

Each session followed a fixed structure with a dual-evaluator protocol (a
Session Lead facilitating, and an independent Observer taking fieldnotes):

| Phase | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Document tour | ~7 min | Participant introduces their own course materials and instructional context |
| Think-aloud interaction | ~25 min | Participant uses the assistant with their own materials, narrating their reasoning aloud |
| Reflective interview | ~12 min | Semi-structured interview (see question bank below) |
| Closing | ~6 min | Wrap-up, post-session survey administration, member-check of key statements |

## B.3 Interview Question Bank

Organized into five categories mapped directly to the paper's research
questions, with dynamic follow-up probes used as needed:

1. **Workflows** — How does this compare to how you currently find/verify
   information in your teaching materials? Where, if anywhere, would this
   fit into your existing workflow?
2. **Accuracy judgments** — What did you rely on to judge whether an answer
   was trustworthy? Can you point to a specific moment where you checked
   (or wished you could check) a claim?
3. **Failure consequences** — What would go wrong if this tool gave a
   confidently wrong answer to you, or to a student, in your context?
4. **Trust calibration** — Under what conditions would you trust an answer
   without independently verifying it? What would change that?
5. **Adoption barriers** — What would you need — technically, procedurally,
   institutionally — before using this regularly, or before letting
   students use it directly?

## B.4 Observer Fieldnote Template

The independent Observer used a structured template tracking, at the
phase level: visible trust shifts (participant statements or behavior
suggesting increased/decreased confidence), hesitation or friction points,
repair strategies the participant used unprompted (e.g., rephrasing,
cross-checking against source material), and an explicit
observation-vs-interpretation label on each note (to separate what was
directly observed from the Observer's inference about its meaning).
