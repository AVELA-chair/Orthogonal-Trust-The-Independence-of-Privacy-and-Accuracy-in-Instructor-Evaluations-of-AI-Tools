# Appendix B: Qualitative Protocols and Question Banks

## B.1 Participant Onboarding

A participant primer was distributed 48 hours before each session. The primer introduced the core concepts that participants would encounter during the study:

- **Socratic facilitation:** Providing scaffolded guidance rather than immediately supplying complete solutions.
- **Document grounding:** Generating responses using information retrieved from the participant’s uploaded course materials.
- **Local/on-device inference:** Processing prompts and documents on the participant’s device without transmitting them to a cloud-based model provider.
- **Parametric information and hallucination:** Information produced from the model’s previously learned parameters, including information that was not present in the uploaded materials or that could not be verified against those materials.

The primer was intended to establish a shared vocabulary. It did not instruct participants how to rate the system or determine whether a response should be trusted.

## B.2 Session Structure and Timing

Each participant completed an approximately 60-minute individual remote session. A Session Lead facilitated the interaction and interview, while a second member of the research team served as Observer and recorded structured, timestamped fieldnotes. After the participant left the session, the Session Lead and Observer conducted an approximately 10-minute research debrief.

| Phase | Duration | Activity | Data Collected |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Welcome, consent, and pre-session survey | ~8 min | Verbal consent was confirmed, session goals were introduced, and the pre-session survey was administered. | Consent confirmation and pre-session survey responses |
| Document tour and setup | ~7 min | The participant was introduced to the system interface and uploaded domain-specific materials, such as syllabi, lecture notes, assignments, or technical documentation. | Document types and contextual notes |
| Observed interaction and think-aloud | ~25 min | The participant queried the system using their own materials while thinking aloud and evaluating the accuracy, relevance, and pedagogical quality of its responses. | Chat logs, screen recordings, think-aloud transcript, and Observer fieldnotes |
| Reflective interview | ~12 min | The Session Lead conducted a semi-structured interview addressing trust, accuracy assessment, pedagogical fit, privacy perceptions, and adoption conditions. | Interview transcript and Observer fieldnotes |
| Post-session ratings and statement confirmation | ~6 min | The participant completed the 30-item Likert-scale instrument and open-ended reflective questions and was invited to confirm or clarify key statements made during the session. | Likert ratings, open-ended responses, and participant clarifications |
| Close recording | ~2 min | Final questions were addressed, the participant was thanked, and the recording was stopped. | — |
| Post-session researcher debrief | ~10 min | After the participant left, the Session Lead and Observer reviewed their notes and documented immediate analytic observations. | Observer notes and researcher reflexive memo |

The participant-facing phases lasted approximately 60 minutes. The post-session researcher debrief was conducted separately and was not included in the participant session duration.

## B.3 Core Interaction Phases

Three phases formed the core of the observed interaction and produced the primary qualitative evidence analyzed in the study.

1. **Document Grounding Tour (~7 minutes).** Participants uploaded their own course PDFs, including lecture slides, assignment specifications, syllabi, and technical documentation. They introduced the instructional context of those materials and conducted an initial grounding check to assess whether the system accurately summarized or represented the uploaded content.

2. **Observed Interaction and Think-Aloud (~25 minutes).** Participants queried the system using their own curricular materials while verbalizing their reasoning and reactions following a think-aloud protocol (Ericsson & Simon, 1993). The Session Lead used non-directive prompts only when a participant stopped verbalizing or required clarification about the task. The Observer recorded notable prompts, system outputs, behavioral hesitation, verification attempts, and breakdown–repair sequences.

3. **Reflective Semi-Structured Interview (~12 minutes).** The Session Lead administered targeted prompts from a five-category question bank aligned with the study’s research questions. These prompts examined participants’ evaluation of response accuracy, anticipated consequences of system failure, trust-calibration practices, privacy perceptions, pedagogical suitability, and conditions for deployment (Kvale & Brinkmann, 2009).

## B.4 Interview Question Bank

The semi-structured interview question bank was organized into five categories aligned with the paper’s research questions. The Session Lead could use follow-up probes when clarification or elaboration was needed.

### B.4.1 Workflows

- How does this compare with how you currently find or verify information in your teaching materials?
- Where, if anywhere, would this system fit into your existing instructional workflow?
- Which tasks would you consider appropriate or inappropriate for this system?
- Did using your own course materials affect how you interacted with the system?

### B.4.2 Accuracy Judgments

- What did you rely on to determine whether a response was trustworthy?
- Can you identify a moment when you verified, or wanted to verify, a system-generated claim?
- How did you determine whether information came from your uploaded documents?
- What kinds of errors were easiest or most difficult to recognize?

### B.4.3 Failure Consequences

- What could happen if this system gave a confidently incorrect answer to you?
- What could happen if it gave such an answer directly to a student?
- Are some types of errors more consequential than others in your instructional context?
- What safeguards would be necessary to reduce those risks?

### B.4.4 Trust Calibration

- Under what conditions would you trust a response without independently verifying it?
- What features or evidence would increase your confidence in a response?
- What would cause you to reduce or withdraw that trust?
- Did local processing affect your trust in the system’s accuracy, privacy, or both?

### B.4.5 Adoption Barriers

- What would you need technically, procedurally, or institutionally before using this system regularly?
- What would need to change before you would permit students to use it directly?
- What forms of documentation, training, or oversight would be required?
- What factors would prevent adoption even if the system operated entirely on-device?

## B.5 Observer Fieldnote Protocol

The Observer used a structured fieldnote template organized by session phase. Notes were timestamped when possible and distinguished direct observation from researcher interpretation.

The template included the following categories:

- **Prompt and response events:** Notable participant prompts, system responses, and follow-up exchanges.
- **Verification behavior:** Attempts to compare responses with uploaded materials, external sources, code execution, or participants’ domain knowledge.
- **Visible trust shifts:** Statements or behaviors suggesting increased or decreased confidence in the system.
- **Hesitation and friction:** Pauses, uncertainty, interface difficulties, repeated questions, or expressions of confusion.
- **Breakdowns:** Incorrect, irrelevant, unsupported, incomplete, or pedagogically inappropriate system responses.
- **Repair strategies:** Participant-initiated actions such as rephrasing, narrowing a prompt, uploading additional material, requesting evidence, manually checking a claim, or abandoning a query.
- **Privacy and data-boundary statements:** Comments concerning local processing, cloud transmission, student data, instructor materials, or institutional requirements.
- **Pedagogical judgments:** Statements concerning scaffolding, answer disclosure, usefulness for students, alignment with course expectations, or required instructor oversight.

Each fieldnote was labeled as either:

- **Observation:** A directly witnessed statement, action, or system event.
- **Interpretation:** The Observer’s provisional explanation of the possible meaning or significance of that event.

Interpretive notes were treated as analytic observations rather than participant statements and were subsequently examined alongside transcripts, chat logs, survey responses, and researcher memos during the cross-case analysis.

## B.6 Protocol Data Sources

The complete protocol produced the following data streams:

1. Pre-session baseline survey responses  
2. Audio and video recordings  
3. Screen-share recordings  
4. Verbatim think-aloud and interview transcripts  
5. Raw system chat logs  
6. Structured Observer fieldnotes  
7. Post-session Likert-scale and open-ended survey responses  
8. Researcher reflexive memos and participant statement-confirmation notes  

These sources were triangulated during the qualitative and cross-case analyses described in the main paper.
